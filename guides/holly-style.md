# Heartwarming Holly house style

Updated after Randy's feedback and reference frames: no neon, no plastic clothes, no Pixar or claymation. The look is a realistic game-cutscene 3D render with real fabric, and "Heartwarming Holly" shows up around the scenes.

## The look (from Randy's reference frames)
Realistic 3D render in the style of a game cutscene or DAZ3D/Unreal character render, the look "My Story"-type animated channels use. Not Pixar, not cartoon, not claymation. Realistic adult proportions and faces with slightly smooth CG skin; real clothes with visible fabric texture (cotton tees, tank tops, denim, suits, jerseys); simple, clean, everyday sets (kitchen, bedroom, office, gym bench) with a few props; natural even lighting with soft shadows; normal true-to-life colors, no neon and no heavy warm tint. Medium and wide shots, eye-level camera, like a video game cinematic.

## Character image style (start of every character prompt)
Realistic 3D character render in a video game cutscene / DAZ3D style, realistic human proportions and face, slightly smooth CG skin, real clothing with visible fabric texture and natural folds, natural true-to-life colors, no neon, not cartoon, not Pixar, not claymation.

## Character image ending (end of every character prompt)
Neutral expression, standing, full body, facing camera, plain light grey studio background, soft even lighting, vertical 9:16.

## Clip style (start of every video prompt)
Realistic 3D animated video in a video game cutscene / DAZ3D render style, realistic human proportions and faces with slightly smooth CG skin, real clothing with fabric texture, simple clean everyday set with a few props, natural even lighting with soft shadows, true-to-life colors (no neon, no heavy warm tint), eye-level camera, vertical 9:16, subtle camera push-in, no subtitles, no captions, no dialogue, ambient sound only. The only readable text in the scene is "HEARTWARMING HOLLY" or "HOLLY" where described.

## Branding: "Heartwarming Holly" around the scenes
Put the brand in 2–3 places per short, always on something that belongs in the scene:
- **Clothing:** a ball cap or t-shirt with a small "HEARTWARMING HOLLY" print, an apron or name tag with "HOLLY"
- **Signs:** a storefront, diner or food-truck sign reading "HEARTWARMING HOLLY", or "HOLLY'S DINER" / "HOLLY'S PIZZA"
- **Props:** a coffee mug, a bumper sticker, a cardboard box, a grocery bag, a newspaper headline
- **Food:** "HOLLY" in frosting, sprinkles or olives
- **Sports and events:** a jersey, banner, scoreboard or ticket: "HOLLY ARENA", "HEARTWARMING HOLLY"

Rules:
- On small things (a shirt print, mug, cap or name tag in a full-body shot), use just **"HOLLY"**. Tested: "HOLLY" comes out clean, but small "HEARTWARMING" gets garbled.
- Use the full **"HEARTWARMING HOLLY"** only where it's big in the frame: a storefront sign, a banner, a truck side, or a close-up of the shirt or mug.
- Video models still garble long words sometimes. For a clip, use the full name only when it's large and in its own close-up; otherwise use "HOLLY". Make the branded thing the start frame, so the text comes from the image.
- If it comes out misspelled, regenerate that shot or cut around it.
- The full name also stays in the watermark, which the editing tool adds.
