## The look in plain words
Looks like a sports video game cutscene (realistic game engine), not an animated movie.
- Faces: slender and long, real adult/teen proportions, defined jaw and cheekbones,
  small natural eyes, thin lips. NOT round, NOT soft, NOT chubby cheeks, NOT big eyes.
- Expressions: almost expressionless. Neutral, flat, a little serious. Mouths mostly closed.
  The story comes from what people DO (hand a towel, put a hand on a shoulder), not faces.
  No big smiles, no crying faces, no hugs, no gasping, no "aww" looks.
- Bodies: realistic, slim, a little stiff and awkward. Arms slightly long, knees slightly bent.
- Skin: smooth matte game-engine skin. Hair as solid sculpted shapes.
- Clothing: realistic, muted colors (whites, navy, greys, browns). Nothing neon or candy-bright.
- Camera: picked per scene. Use it to add motion where the scene needs energy (someone
  complaining, making a point): orbit around them (half or full circle) while pushing
  in and out. Calmer moments get a simple slow zoom in or out.
  Mostly medium and wide shots with 2-3 people in frame and a real background
  (crowd, room, street). Avoid face close-ups; at most one close-up per video.
- Lighting: realistic indoor/outdoor light, not golden-hour glow on everything.

## User notes (Olive redo, Nov 27)
- Think POSABLE MANNEQUIN: a jointed figure. The acting is done by bending the joints
  (head forward/back/left/right, shoulders, elbows, torso, waist, hips, knees), i.e. clear
  body poses: pointing, hands on hips, slumping forward, arms thrown up, turning, walking off.
  The face, head size, ears, body size and proportions NEVER change from shot to shot.
  The body tells the emotion; the face stays the same.
- MOVEMENT = ROBOTIC, NOT ZOMBIE: crisp, mechanical, joint-by-joint moves, like a posable
  figure or robot. Each joint moves on its own, snaps into a pose, holds still, then the next
  move. NOT slow, limp, shuffling, swaying or droopy (zombie). Put in every video prompt:
  "robotic mechanical joint-by-joint movement, crisp moves that snap into poses and hold,
  not slow, not limp, not zombie-like".
- NO expression at all. Blank, neutral faces the whole time.
- Thinner people, lighter skin tone than the drifted versions.
- Posture: slightly hunched. One or two characters stand with a forward "C" curve:
  chest and hips pushed out further than the middle, a slightly swayed, awkward stance.
- Semi low-poly look: you can see slight flat planes on faces, hair and clothes (like an
  older game engine), not smooth and soft.
- Simple plain clothes. No fancy details.
- Characters drift over a video: expressions grow, colors change, bodies shrink and round.
  Stop it: every clip uses the ORIGINAL approved stills as references, never a frame from
  an earlier clip, and every clip gets compared to the stills before it's used.

- EYES: bright, glowing, light-colored eyes (crystal blue / pale green) that stand out. A must.
  See GOOD_wendy_chef_eyes.jpg.
- COLOR of the whole video: warm, soft, slightly hazy and washed-out, creamy whites, warm
  browns and wood tones, low contrast. See GOOD_wendy_chef_color.jpg.
- ARMS/SHOULDERS: a little weird on purpose: long arms, broad sloped shoulders, rubbery joints.

## Spending rule
1. Before any video: make ONE still (2 credits) of each character in this style and compare it
   side by side with GOOD_holly_towel.jpg. User approves before anything else.
2. Every video gets the approved character stills AND GOOD_holly_towel.jpg as references.
3. Make Scene 1 only, check it against the reference, show the user, then the rest.
4. Re-check every 3 clips against the reference picture, not against the last clip.
