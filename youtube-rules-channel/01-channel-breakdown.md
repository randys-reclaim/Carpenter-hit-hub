## 3. The repeatable template (beat sheet)

Timings come from the 8:08 clock video. Scale them proportionally for shorter cuts.

| Time | Beat | What happens | Example line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | **Title card** | The title is read aloud over the hero image. The command is shown in red. | "If your clock ever says 25:00… stay in bed." |
| 0:04–0:12 | **Rapid-fire teaser** | 3–4 rules flashed at one second each, out of context. This creates curiosity gaps. | "Don't check your phone. Don't sign anything." |
| 0:12–0:16 | **The contract** | "There are seven rules." Six keep you safe; the seventh *explains* something. | "Six will get you home. The seventh explains why you were late." |
| 0:16–1:10 | **Rule 1 + grounding** | A real fact first ("That's official. You can look it up."), then the slide into the impossible. | Footsteps / "the house settling" |
| ~1:00 | **Anecdote** | "A man in Phoenix got up to check." A specific city plus a bland, unsettling outcome. | "He'd folded everything." |
| 1:10–6:10 | **Rules 2–6** | About 50–60s each. Every rule ends by **re-explaining a real everyday annoyance** as evidence. | Lost keys, missing socks, the snooze button, dreams fading, why the dog doesn't bark |
| ~3:45 | **Mid-video "break"** | A pause beat that resets attention. | "At 25:30, everything stops. That's the break. Ten minutes." |
| 6:10–7:40 | **Rule 7 = the twist** | The viewer *is* part of the anomaly. Earlier "explanations" are recalled as proof. | "You've seen this file before." |
| 7:40–8:08 | **Agency sign-off** | A cold, institutional final line. This builds the lore. | "We are a trusted government agency. We make the schedule." |

### Writing rules he follows
1. **Second person, present/future tense.** "You'll hear footsteps." "They'll pass your door." The viewer is the main character.
2. **Ultra-short lines (3–8 words).** Each one becomes one caption card. Lots of fragments: "Halfway." "Staring." "First try."
3. **Flat, calm delivery.** A government-PSA tone, never a "scary voice". Horror comes from understatement.
4. **Mundane-to-impossible ladder.** Every rule starts with something true and slowly bends it.
5. **The "that's why" payoff.** Each rule explains a real-life annoyance ("That's why dreams vanish minutes after you wake up"). This is the **#1 engagement trick**: viewers comment their own experiences.
6. **Callbacks.** Lines from the cold open come back in the twist ("Long night.").
7. **One dry joke per rule.** "Drivers hate that." "By now it would be rude not to." This keeps it watchable rather than bleak.
8. **No gore, no jump scares.** It's family-safe and advertiser-friendly, and kids and teens can share it.

## 4. Why it gets so many views

1. **Title formula = search + curiosity in one line.**
   `If Your [EVERYDAY THING] Ever [IMPOSSIBLE DETAIL]… [ALL-CAPS COMMAND]`
   - The *everyday thing* (dog, phone, Uber, clock) is a high-volume keyword people already search and that YouTube already associates with them.
   - The *impossible detail* (101%, 25:00, no SIM card) is a specific number or wrongness that makes people click to resolve it.
   - The *command* (DO NOT UNPLUG IT) implies danger *and* insider knowledge.
2. **Thumbnails are instantly readable at phone size.** Two words (white + red), one icon, black or red background. They can be read in 0.3 seconds in a feed.
3. **It rides the "rules horror" wave.** This genre (rule lists, analog horror, creepypasta, the "Backrooms" ecosystem) has a huge young audience on YouTube and TikTok.
4. **Universal relatability.** Everyone has lost a sock, hit snooze, or had a dog stare at them. The video "explains" it, viewers comment their stories, comments push engagement, and YouTube pushes the video.
5. **Retention design.** Seven rules equals seven open loops. Progress pips at the top show how far there is to go, and the twist is held for the final 90 seconds. Line-by-line captions also hold people watching on mute.
6. **Binge-able series.** Same format, same narrator, and a shared "agency" lore. One hit feeds the others, and "File 053" numbering implies a big archive.
7. **Cheap to make.** Flat vector stick figures, simple motion, one voice. That allows daily uploads, and more uploads means more lottery tickets for a 2M outlier.
8. **Watch time per view.** 5–9 minutes clears the 4–8 minute sweet spot for ads. One mid-roll is possible at 8+ minutes.
