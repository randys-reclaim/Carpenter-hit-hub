If Your Furnace Ever Turns On In July… DON'T TOUCH THE THERMOSTAT

If your furnace ever turns on in July…
leave it.
Don't touch the thermostat.
Don't open the fridge after midnight.
And if there's a breaker in your panel you didn't label… don't flip it.
Seven rules.
The seventh explains why your house was built for one more person than lives in it.
Rule one. Don't touch the thermostat.
It's ninety-four degrees outside.
At 2:10 a.m., you'll hear it start.
A click in the wall. A hum. That's the inducer motor clearing the flue.
A ticking. That's the igniter.
Then the whoosh.
That's all real. Any HVAC man can tell you the order.
What they'll also tell you is the blower waits about a minute before it pushes air.
So it doesn't blow cold on you.
That's the official reason.
The real reason is it's warming up the room first.
Not your room.
You'll want to turn it off.
The thermostat will already say OFF.
It's been saying OFF since May.
A woman in Mesquite pulled the wire off the wall.
The furnace kept running.
In the morning, every vent in her house was cold.
Except one. In a closet.
She doesn't use that closet.
She found a blanket in there. Folded.
Rule two. Don't open the fridge after midnight.
Every eight to twelve hours, your fridge heats itself.
A little element melts the frost off the coils.
You'll hear dripping. A sizzle.
It's called the defrost cycle. It's in your manual.
The manual doesn't say what it's thawing.
You'll hear it hum. Then stop. Then hum again. In a rhythm. In. Out.
Your fridge doesn't breathe.
Something standing behind it does. The hum is to cover it.
If you open the door, the light comes on.
The light reaches the floor. The floor reaches the gap behind the fridge.
The gap is about four inches. That's enough. That's always been enough.
Close your bedroom door.
In the morning, the milk will be in the back. You didn't put it there.
It doesn't like the light either.
Rule three. Don't follow the ticking in the ducts.
Metal ductwork grows when it heats up and shrinks when it cools.
It pops. It ticks. It bangs. Contractors call it oil canning. It's normal.
What's not normal is when the ticking moves.
Kitchen. Hallway.
The vent over your bed.
It stops there.
Ducts don't stop.
A man in Tulsa took the cover off his bedroom register with a screwdriver. Just to look.
He said the duct was warm. Like a hand.
He put the cover back on.
In the morning, all four screws were tighter than he left them.
From the inside.
Rule four. If the AC drips inside, don't wipe it up.
Your air conditioner pulls water out of the air. Gallons a day.
It runs down a little pipe called the condensate line.
When that line clogs, water backs up into your house.
That's the most common service call in Texas. Look it up.
What techs don't put on the ticket is what's in the water.
Footprints. Bare. Wet. From the air handler to your bedroom door. Then back.
They're smaller than yours.
They're getting bigger.
If you wipe them up, it'll know you saw them.
Leave the towel where it is.
It dries faster than water should.
You've noticed all of this before.
You called it an old house. You called it a new house.
You called a guy.
We sent the guy.
Rule five. Leave the water heater alone.
Hard water leaves sediment at the bottom of the tank. When the burner heats it, it pops. Like knocking.
The manual says drain it once a year. Don't.
Count the knocks. One for every person home.
You live alone. It knocks twice.
A family in Lubbock drained theirs.
The sediment came out gray. In the bucket, it moved.
Their water's been hot ever since.
They never turn on the heater.
Rule six. Don't flip the breaker you didn't label.
Open your panel. The labels are in somebody's handwriting.
Kitchen. Dryer. Master.
One says HIS ROOM.
Your house doesn't have his room.
That breaker is always warm.
If you flip it, the house goes quiet. No fridge. No furnace. No AC. Nothing.
That's the first time you'll hear it.
It was never the appliances making noise.
The appliances were the only thing keeping it quiet.
A landlord in Waco flipped it to rewire. He lasted four minutes. He flipped it back.
He never rented that unit again.
He still pays the electric on it. Three hundred dollars a month.
Nobody lives there.
When a contractor sizes your heating and air, he uses a form. It's called Manual J.
It counts square feet. Windows. Insulation. And people.
The rule is bedrooms, plus one. A three-bedroom house is sized for four. Every house in America.
That's real. Ask your HVAC guy.
Ask him who the plus one is.
He'll say it's a guest. Watch his face when he says it.
Rule seven. Keep the house warm.
It was here before the house. The house was built around it.
The furnace is for it. The fridge hum is for it.
The ticking is it, moving room to room to stay near the heat.
It doesn't want you. It wants warm.
As long as the house is warm, it stays in the walls.
When the house gets cold, it finds the warmest thing inside.
That's why some nights you can't get warm. Under three blankets. Shaking.
That's not a fever.
That's company.
Every click at 2 a.m. Every tick in the ceiling.
Every milk carton that moved to the back.
Every electric bill higher than your neighbor's.
Same size house. Same street.
You've been heating for two.

don't light a candle. Don't open the fridge. Sit still. And hum. Low. Steady. Like a fridge.
It'll think the house is still running. It'll stay where it is.
